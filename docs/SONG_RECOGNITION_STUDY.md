# Study: improving song recognition (2026-10-21)

Question: «Reconocer canción» ("Recognise song") (`POST /api/assist/listen`) gets the genre right for 3 out of every 5 songs. Can it be
improved with more audio measurements or with a tagging model that gives hints to the assistant? Everything was tested outside the
application; **the recognition has not been changed**.

## Method

- 9 songs generated by us, whose requested style is known (`scripts/study-song-recognition/songs.tsv`):
  cyberpunk synth-pop, shanty folk, chamber indie pop, romantic wedding piano, techno rap, jazz, tavern folk,
  piano ballad and film music. One 25 s clip of each.
- Each hint variant is given to the same `qwen3:8b` on CPU with the prompt of the `listen` task, without lyrics (to isolate
  the audio). A person judged the result by reading the genre it answered: **n = 9, no confidence interval**; it is useful
  for spotting large differences, not small ones. The thresholds of the new measurements were set by looking at those same
  songs.

## Results

| Hint the assistant receives | Genres correct (out of 9) |
|---|---|
| The current one (centroid, bass, flatness, dynamics, onsets) | 2 |
| Current + rolloff, harmonic/percussive ratio, pulse regularity | 2 |
| Those measurements instead of «dark / bass-heavy / loud» | 2 |
| Current + 3 genres and instruments from MuQ-MuLan | ~6 |
| New measurements + MuQ-MuLan | ~6-7 |
| Current + AST (AudioSet) labels | ~3 |

- **More measurements do not help.** They separate the songs well (rolloff at 85 %: ~1.200 Hz in the film music, ~1.800 in the jazz, ~6.300
  in the cyberpunk; harmonic/percussive: 61 in the film music, 2 in the cyberpunk; spectral contrast separates little), but the
  assistant does not know how to go from those figures to a genre and, with them, the indie song became "drum and bass". The underlying flaw
  of the current method is that almost all acoustic music comes out as "dark, bass-heavy and loud" (the energy is always
  in the low end) and the assistant mistakes it for electronic music.
- **MuQ-MuLan does help as a hint**, even though it is only slightly accurate (it measures badly, with a bias towards "funk"): the assistant
  corrects its mistakes using the measurements. It fails on the wedding song and the piano ballad. Without the assistant's hint, MuQ gets the
  genre right for 3 of the 5 songs of the first trial.
- **LAION-CLAP** (`laion/clap-htsat-unfused`; the `larger_clap_music` checkpoint gives the same text embedding for
  any phrase and is unusable in transformers) does not distinguish genres: all scores between 0,5 and 0,67.
- **AST** (`MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593`, BSD-3) labels the jazz as "Christmas music" and the techno rap as
  reggae; it only gets the mood right (sad, tender) for the ballads and the film music. Its genre probabilities are low
  (0,03-0,2): it was trained on real clips and generated music is far from them.

## Licences and costs

| Model | Weights licence | Size | CPU time |
|---|---|---|---|
| MuQ-MuLan | CC-BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial) | ~700M parameters | ~10 s per 25 s clip |
| LAION-CLAP | Apache-2.0 | ~600 MB | ~2 s |
| AST | BSD-3 | ~350 MB | a few seconds |

MuQ-MuLan is already on disk: DiffRhythm2 uses it to turn its style text into a vector (text branch only)
and it is in `models/music/diffrhythm2`. If it were connected to the recognition, the natural way would be to add a call to the
DiffRhythm2 service instead of another service, at the cost of starting a heavier one.

## Conclusion

The only thing that really improves the genre is a MuQ-MuLan hint, but its weights do not allow commercial use; neither CLAP nor
AST contribute anything. As long as it is not used commercially, connecting MuQ as a hint to the assistant is the available improvement; for
commercial use one would have to look for another model or train a classifier of our own with generated songs. With the
application as it is, the LAION-CLAP service stays as a parked experiment.
